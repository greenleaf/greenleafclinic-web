Weight Loss

Weight Management

Weight gain is rarely just "eating too much and moving too little." Two people can eat the same way and gain weight very differently, because appetite, digestion, fluid balance, stress hormones, and metabolic rate don't work the same way in everyone. We start by finding which pattern is driving your weight, then treat that.

Why weight management isn't one-size-fits-all

If you've ever felt like the same diet and exercise advice just doesn't work for you the way it seems to for someone else, there's a reason. Appetite signals, digestion speed, how your body handles fluid and waste, stress hormones, and age-related changes in metabolic rate all vary from person to person — and they all affect how easily weight comes on and how hard it is to lose.

At Greenleaf, we don't treat weight gain as a single problem with a single fix. We look at how your particular body is losing its metabolic balance, then build a plan around that. Below are six patterns we see most often in clinic. Most people don't fit neatly into just one — two or three overlapping patterns is common — and a proper assessment happens during your visit.

Six common patterns behind weight gain

Pattern 01

Appetite-Driven Type

Digestion runs fast and strong here, but the signal that's supposed to tell you when to stop eating isn't keeping up — so meals turn into overeating. This shows up most often in younger, more active people, and weight tends to climb quickly once it starts.

Common signs

  • Frequently hungry, craving rich or sweet food
  • Weight gain concentrated around the upper body and abdomen
  • Bad breath or gums that swell and bleed easily
  • Tendency toward constipation, heartburn, or bloating
Treatment focus

Calming the overactive appetite signal and cooling the digestive system, while helping the gut clear waste more efficiently.

Pattern 02

Fluid Retention Type

This is the "I gain weight just drinking water" pattern — the body's ability to process fluid and clear waste has slowed down. It's common in people with naturally weaker digestion, or whose metabolism has been worn down by irregular eating and repeated dieting.

Common signs

  • Soft, puffy weight gain — face and hands often swollen in the morning
  • Heavy, sluggish feeling; full after eating only a small amount
  • Weight that won't budge despite eating very little
  • Loose stools or frequent diarrhea
Treatment focus

Restoring digestive function so the body can regulate fluid normally again, and clearing the fluid buildup causing the swelling.

Pattern 03

Metabolic Syndrome Type

When fluid retention goes on long enough, waste products can build up in the blood vessels and tissues themselves. This pattern is common in abdominal obesity that comes together with high cholesterol, fatty liver, or insulin resistance.

Common signs

  • Fat concentrated around the abdomen; visibly protruding belly
  • Chest tightness, excess phlegm, or reduced appetite
  • Heavy or numb limbs, foggy head, dizziness
  • Excessive sweating
Treatment focus

Clearing built-up waste from the blood vessels and tissues while supporting fat metabolism, alongside monitoring related markers such as blood sugar and lipids.

Pattern 04

Stress-Driven Type

Ongoing stress or chronic tension throws the nervous system out of balance, which slows metabolism down. This pattern shows up closely with stress-triggered overeating and the yo-yo cycle of repeated dieting followed by regain.

Common signs

  • Reaches for food immediately when stressed
  • Upper body runs warm with facial flushing, while the lower body stays cold and swollen
  • Tightness in the sides or chest; strong PMS symptoms
  • Unsettled sleep
Treatment focus

Restoring nervous system balance to ease the stress-driven urge to eat, and releasing the tension that's been holding the body's functions in a stuck pattern.

Pattern 05

Slow Metabolism Type

As metabolic rate naturally declines with age and digestion slows alongside it, weight gain follows. This is the pattern most commonly seen in gradual midlife weight gain, weight gain around menopause, and weight gain linked to an underactive thyroid.

Common signs

  • Gradual, full-body weight gain with noticeably reduced muscle tone
  • Very sensitive to cold, especially cold hands and feet
  • Low energy, sore knees or lower back, frequent nighttime urination
  • In women, periods that become very light or stop
Treatment focus

Raising overall metabolic function and restoring the body's ability to stay warm and circulate properly.

Pattern 06

Circulation-Related Type

When any of the patterns above go on long enough, circulation itself can become sluggish. This pattern goes beyond weight alone — it calls for attention to cardiovascular health as well — and weight loss is often especially slow to respond here.

Common signs

  • Firmer weight gain, but with dull or dark skin tone
  • Bruises easily
  • Heavy periods with clotting and significant cramping
  • Noticeably dark or dusky lip colour
Treatment focus

Improving circulation throughout the body. Where cardiovascular risk factors are involved, we recommend working alongside your physician's care rather than in place of it.

When to see your doctor first. If weight gain may be related to a thyroid condition, diabetes, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), or another underlying endocrine or metabolic condition — or if weight has changed suddenly and without explanation — please check with your physician first to rule out or manage the underlying condition. Treatment at Greenleaf is not a substitute for that diagnosis or medical care; it works alongside it to support overall metabolic balance.

Frequently asked questions

Can acupuncture and herbal medicine actually cause weight loss?

The goal isn't to "burn off" weight with herbs. It's to address the underlying factors that are making weight difficult to manage — fluid retention, sluggish digestion, stress-driven overeating — so your body is better able to reach and hold a healthy weight. Results are typically better when combined with attention to diet and activity.

How long before I notice a difference?

This varies a lot depending on your pattern, constitution, and how long the weight gain has been building. Most people notice changes in how they feel first — less swelling, easier digestion, more even appetite — within about four to eight weeks of treatment, with weight change tending to follow after those underlying shifts.

I've dieted repeatedly and keep regaining the weight. Can this help?

Yes — repeated dieting and rebound weight gain are closely tied to a slowed metabolism and stress-driven eating patterns. Rather than restricting food intake further, treatment focuses on rebuilding metabolic function and calming the stress response, which tends to hold up better over the long term than cutting calories alone.

Should I get acupuncture, herbal medicine, or both?

It depends on your pattern and situation. Acupuncture tends to help most with nervous system balance and circulation, while herbal medicine is used to correct constitutional metabolic imbalances. Many people benefit from both together — your practitioner will recommend an approach after your assessment.

I gained weight suddenly after menopause. Is that just something I have to accept?

The main driver of weight gain around menopause is the drop in metabolic rate that comes with hormonal change — but that slowdown can still be supported and managed. Improving digestion and circulation together can help slow the rate of weight gain and support your overall sense of wellbeing during this stage.

Is there anything I should watch for during treatment?

Keeping regular meal times and a consistent sleep schedule matters more than cutting food intake drastically. It's helpful to notice changes in digestion, bowel movements, and swelling as treatment progresses and share them with us at your visits — that information helps us adjust your treatment plan.
