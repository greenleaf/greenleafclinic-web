How Traditional Eastern Medicine Views Adenomyosis

Adenomyosis happens when the tissue that normally lines the uterus grows into the muscular wall of the uterus itself, often causing intense menstrual pain and heavy bleeding that disrupts daily life.

In Traditional Eastern Medicine, adenomyosis isn't treated as an isolated, local problem. It's understood as the result of long-term poor circulation and chronic tissue congestion that gradually settles deep into the uterine muscle over time.

Because the changes are spread diffusely through the muscle layer rather than confined to one spot, treatment doesn't aim to physically remove a lesion. Instead, the goal is to shift the circulation pattern across the whole uterus so tissue stops accumulating, pain eases, and the body's own capacity to recover takes over.

Based on your constitution and how your symptoms show up, we look at five different circulation patterns behind how your pain and bleeding developed.

Five Patterns Behind Your Symptoms

Pattern 1

Stress-Related Circulation Pattern

Common in patients under heavy emotional stress or with a tendency to hold in frustration. Pain is typically most intense right before and at the very start of your period.

Chronic stress keeps your nervous system on alert, which constricts blood vessels in the pelvis. Over time, blood can't circulate properly through the uterine muscle, and congestion builds up locally. When your period starts and the uterus contracts, that contraction collides with the congested tissue — causing sharp lower abdominal pain.

Common symptoms
  • Bloating, ballooning pain in the lower abdomen starting 1–2 days before your period
  • Tightness or fullness in the sides and breasts
  • Pain ranging from stabbing to a bursting sensation
  • Dark, clotted menstrual blood; pain eases somewhat once clots pass
  • Noticeable mood swings, low mood, irritability
Pattern 2

Cold-Circulation Pattern

Common in those with chronic cold sensitivity or a preference for cold food and drink, or with incomplete recovery after a miscarriage or uterine procedure.

When the pelvic area stays chronically cold, blood vessels stay constricted and blood flow slows. During your period, this constriction intensifies, the uterine muscle tightens sharply, and it collides with congested tissue — producing cramping pain.

Common symptoms
  • Severe menstrual pain that improves noticeably with heat
  • Persistently cold lower abdomen and lower back
  • Periods that come late, are light in flow, dark in color, with clots
  • Watery vaginal discharge, cold hands and feet, frequent clear urination
For this pattern, moxibustion (heat therapy) at points like Guanyuan (CV4), Qihai (CV6), and Zigong (uterus point) is especially effective for warming the area directly.
Pattern 3

Inflammation-Related Circulation Pattern

Common in those with pelvic inflammation or excessively heavy bleeding with ongoing lower abdominal pain.

Repeated local inflammation causes tissue fluids to thicken, making congestion more likely. This congested tissue creates inflammatory pooling within the uterine muscle, driving both pain and heavy bleeding. It tends to progress in stages — starting as localized inflammation, then spreading to surrounding tissue as blood vessels become more permeable, and in more severe cases, involving body-wide fatigue or heat sensations.

Common symptoms
  • Heavy periods with dark red or cloudy-colored blood, sometimes with odor
  • Persistent burning or dull ache in the lower abdomen
  • Yellow discharge and vulvar itching
  • Bitter taste in the mouth, bloating, dark and uncomfortable urination
Pattern 4

Reduced Reproductive Function Pattern

Common in those with a history of multiple births, miscarriages, or repeated uterine procedures, or whose whole-body hormonal balance has weakened around perimenopause.

When the drive behind blood flow to the uterus weakens, circulation through the muscle layer slows and congestion builds more easily. At the same time, the body's ability to regulate menstrual flow declines, so bleeding increases and tissue repair slows, allowing the condition to deepen gradually.

Common symptoms
  • A pulling, dull ache in the lower back and sacrum during your period
  • Heavy periods, or the opposite — very light with a prolonged cycle
  • Dizziness, ringing in the ears, weak lower back and knees, low libido, frequent urination, and fertility difficulties
Pattern 5

Chronic Fatigue Pattern (Low Vitality)

Common in those with weak digestive function, chronic overwork, or anemia from heavy periods, and generally low in overall stamina.

When the body's overall circulatory drive weakens, its ability to regulate blood vessels and keep blood contained also breaks down. The result is a seemingly contradictory picture — circulation slows and congestion builds, while at the same time bleeding doesn't stop and periods become heavier.

Common symptoms
  • Long, heavy periods with pale, watery blood
  • Severe fatigue, pale or sallow complexion
  • Heavy, dragging sensation in the lower abdomen
  • Poor appetite, loose stools, shortness of breath, excessive sweating

Treatment Approach: During Your Period vs. Between Periods

Because adenomyosis tends to cause stronger pain and heavier bleeding than other uterine conditions, treatment is approached differently depending on where you are in your cycle.

During Your Period Symptom relief

The priority is pain relief and controlling heavy bleeding. We work to release congested tissue, but calibrate intensity carefully — too much can make bleeding worse. Acupuncture and moxibustion are used together to maximize pain relief.

Between Periods Root cause

This is when we address the underlying pattern. Treatment is tailored to your specific pattern to regulate whole-body function and normalize circulation to the uterus. Ongoing acupuncture at points like Guanyuan, Qihai, Zigong, and Baliao gradually helps the body absorb the congestion built up deep in the uterine muscle.

We always coordinate with your OB-GYN. Traditional Eastern Medicine treatment for adenomyosis works alongside, not instead of, conventional medical care.

Because adenomyosis is spread diffusely through the uterine muscle, the core of Traditional Eastern Medicine treatment isn't removing a lesion directly — it's changing the circulation environment of the whole uterus so congestion stops building and pain stops being triggered, restoring the body's own balance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Traditional Eastern Medicine treatment fully cure adenomyosis?
Because adenomyosis involves diffuse changes deep within the uterine muscle, treatment isn't about removing it entirely — it's focused on reducing pain and heavy bleeding and slowing how the condition progresses. This varies a lot from person to person, so an accurate assessment of your specific situation comes first.
How long before I notice a difference?
We typically track changes in pain and bleeding over a few menstrual cycles. Acute pain relief is often felt fairly quickly, but real improvement in underlying circulation takes consistent, ongoing care.
Can I still get acupuncture or moxibustion during my period?
Yes — it's often used specifically for pain relief during the times when pain is worst. If bleeding is very heavy, we adjust the intensity of treatment accordingly.
Can I combine this with conventional treatment (hormone therapy, surgery)?
Yes, that's absolutely possible. We'd ask that you discuss combining treatments with your OB-GYN first. If your symptoms are severe or you're dealing with anemia, conventional treatment may need to take priority.
Can I still be treated if I'm planning to get pregnant?
Please let us know in advance if you're planning a pregnancy — it can change both the treatment approach and the herbal formulas we use.
