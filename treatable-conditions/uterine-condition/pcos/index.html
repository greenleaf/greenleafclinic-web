Uterine Condition

PCOS looks different in every patient

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common hormonal conditions in women of reproductive age, typically involving irregular or absent periods, difficulty ovulating, and small cysts on the ovaries — often alongside weight changes, acne, or excess hair growth. But the same diagnosis can come from very different places in the body. We start by identifying which pattern is driving your symptoms, then treat the cause behind it — not just the label.

One diagnosis, many patterns

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is never caused by just one thing. In Traditional Eastern Medicine, it's understood as a state where metabolism, circulation, and hormone signaling are pulled out of balance together — and the way that imbalance shows up can look completely different from one patient to the next.

Below are six patterns we commonly see, described in plain physiological terms. Most people don't fit neatly into just one — but recognizing which pattern is closest to what you're experiencing right now is the first step toward the right kind of support.

Six patterns of PCOS

Pattern 01

Metabolic & Fluid Retention Pattern

The most common pattern, marked by weight gain and puffiness. When metabolic and hormonal regulation are naturally weaker, or years of physical strain build up, the body's fluid and energy metabolism slows down. Fluid and waste products collect in the tissues, circulation slows, and this in turn affects blood flow and hormone signaling around the ovaries and uterus.

Signs your body may be showing

Long or absent menstrual cycles
Weight that doesn't respond to dieting
Puffiness in the face and limbs
Watery discharge, low libido
Cold, achy lower back and knees, easy fatigue

Treatment focus

Treatment restores metabolic and fluid-circulation function to clear the underlying congestion.

Pattern 02

Digestive & Metabolic Stagnation Pattern

Linked to weaker digestion, carb-heavy eating habits, and low activity. When digestion and absorption weaken, the body can't efficiently convert food into usable energy, and unprocessed byproducts build up. Over time this metabolic buildup starts to resemble insulin resistance and begins to affect ovarian and uterine function.

Signs your body may be showing

Lighter periods, delayed cycles
Heavy, sluggish limbs
Poor appetite or binge eating
Loose or sticky stools
Sallow, oily skin with breakouts

Treatment focus

Treatment strengthens digestion and metabolism to help the body clear built-up congestion.

Pattern 03

Stress-Driven Nervous System Pattern

Common even in lean-body PCOS. Chronic stress and emotional tension act directly on hormone rhythms through the nervous system. When nervous system regulation is disrupted, the signaling that controls ovulation becomes irregular, which shows up as cycle irregularity.

Signs your body may be showing

Irregular menstrual cycles
Breast and lower-abdominal bloating before periods
Alternating diarrhea and constipation
Irritability, low mood, frequent sighing
Insomnia

Treatment focus

Treatment restores nervous system balance and eases the body's stress response.

Pattern 04

Chronic Circulation Pattern

Seen in long-standing PCOS with persistent ovulation difficulty. When functional congestion goes on long enough, it starts to affect blood vessels, reducing local circulation. Ongoing poor circulation around the ovaries and uterus makes ovulation difficult, and small cysts tend to persist.

Signs your body may be showing

Periods absent for months, or dark and clotted when they occur
Stabbing lower-abdominal pain, tender to pressure
Dull complexion, bluish lips
Skin prone to dark patches or pigmentation

Treatment focus

Treatment promotes circulation to resolve localized blood-flow congestion.

Pattern 05

Depleted & Dry Pattern

Common in lean body types with dry skin and flushing. Chronic fatigue, poor sleep, and prolonged overexertion deplete the body's resources for producing and regulating hormones. This depletion causes a relative overactivation of the body's stress response, producing low-grade heat sensations that affect the uterine and vascular environment.

Signs your body may be showing

Very light periods or prolonged spotting
Heat sensations in hands, feet, and chest
Flushed cheeks, night sweats
Aching lower back and knees, insomnia
Dry mouth and tongue

Treatment focus

Treatment replenishes the body's reserves and calms excess heat sensations.

Pattern 06

Insulin Resistance & Inflammatory Pattern

Marked by insulin resistance and inflammatory acne. A diet high in greasy, spicy foods tends to generate inflammatory byproducts during metabolism. Over time this affects hormone signaling pathways, driving both cycle irregularity and skin inflammation — acne and excess oil — at the same time.

Signs your body may be showing

Early or heavy periods
Sticky, odorous discharge, vulvar itching
Inflammatory acne on face, chest, back
Bitter, sticky taste in the mouth
Dark urine, feeling of incomplete relief

Treatment focus

Treatment lowers inflammatory activity and improves metabolic circulation.

How we think about treatment

  • 1

    The pattern matters. A weight-predominant case and a lean, depleted case can call for opposite approaches — what helps one can work against the other.

  • 2

    PCOS isn't only an ovary problem. Metabolism, digestion, and the nervous system all factor into the picture, and treatment works best when it addresses the pattern behind the diagnosis, not the diagnosis alone.

  • 3

    Overlap is common. Most patients show two or three patterns together rather than fitting neatly into one, which is why an in-person assessment is what actually determines your treatment plan.

Common questions

How do I know which pattern I am?
Most patients don't fit neatly into just one pattern — two or three often overlap. An in-person assessment is needed for an accurate read on your particular case.
Can Traditional Eastern Medicine cure PCOS?
PCOS involves a complex overlap of hormonal, metabolic, and nervous system factors, so treatment focuses less on a cure and more on restoring circulation and metabolic balance — managing symptoms and improving ovulation and cycle regularity. Response speed and degree vary by individual.
Can I combine this with conventional treatment (hormone medication, insulin-sensitizing drugs, etc.)?
Yes, combining approaches is common. If you're currently on medication or have an active treatment plan, it's important to keep both your physician and your practitioner here informed.
How long does treatment take?
This varies by constitution, symptom severity, and lifestyle, so it's hard to give a single answer. Because menstrual cycles are involved, most patients need at least 2 to 3 months of observation.
Does lifestyle matter?
Very much. Diet, sleep, and stress management directly affect treatment outcomes — especially for metabolic and circulation-related patterns, where results are limited without lifestyle changes alongside treatment.
