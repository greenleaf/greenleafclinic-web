Uterine Condition

Uterine fibroids form for different reasons

Uterine fibroids are benign growths in the uterine muscle wall, and they're extremely common in women of reproductive age. Even with the same diagnosis, the background each patient's fibroids grew from — and the signals the body is sending — can look very different. We look beyond the mass itself, to the whole-body circulation and metabolic state that let it form, and treat that underlying pattern rather than the label alone.

One diagnosis, many patterns

Uterine fibroids are never caused by just one thing. In Traditional Eastern Medicine, a fibroid isn't treated as a fixed, isolated mass — it's understood as a sign of a broader circulation and metabolic state in the body that made it possible for tissue to build up in the first place.

Below are five patterns we commonly see, described in plain physiological terms. Most people don't fit neatly into just one — but recognizing which pattern is closest to what you're experiencing right now is the first step toward the right kind of support.

Five patterns of uterine fibroids

Pattern 01

Stress & Circulation Pattern

The most common pattern, closely tied to chronic stress. Ongoing stress and emotional suppression disrupt autonomic nervous system balance and raise tension in pelvic blood vessels and smooth muscle. Over time, blood flow through the pelvis becomes sluggish, blood and tissue fluid stagnate locally, and this stagnation can gradually develop into fibroid tissue.

Signs your body may be showing

Bloating and tension in the lower abdomen, flanks, and chest before periods
Severe menstrual cramps
Dark, clotted menstrual blood
Irregular menstrual cycles
Irritability, frequent low mood

Treatment focus

Treatment focuses on releasing blocked circulation — acupuncture to regulate autonomic balance and promote pelvic blood flow, alongside circulation-supporting herbs that help stagnant fluid and blood move again.

Pattern 02

Cold & Peripheral Circulation Pattern

Common in those who tend to run cold or favor cold food and drink. Ongoing exposure to cold, or a natural decline in internal metabolic function, causes blood vessels to constrict and peripheral circulation to drop. Much like water freezing slowly in cold weather, blood flow through the pelvis slows and tissue stagnates, creating conditions where fibroids form more easily.

Signs your body may be showing

Lower abdomen that always feels cold
Increased clear, watery discharge
Menstrual cramps that ease with warmth
Late periods, scant and dark in colour
Cold hands and feet, aching low back and knees

Treatment focus

Treatment focuses on restoring body warmth and dilating peripheral vessels to improve circulation — warming acupuncture and moxibustion, alongside herbs that warm the body and support circulation so blood flow can reach the pelvic area.

Pattern 03

Metabolic Decline & Fluid Stagnation Pattern

Often seen alongside weight gain, slowed metabolism, and insulin resistance. When digestive and metabolic function decline, the body isn't able to clear water and waste efficiently, and fluid begins to collect between tissues. This stagnant fluid, combined with reduced circulation, often shows up as softer, cystic fibroids.

Signs your body may be showing

A palpable lower-abdominal mass without much pain
Heaviness in the body, easy swelling
Thick, heavy vaginal discharge
Oily face and scalp
Chest tightness, nausea

Treatment focus

Treatment restores fluid metabolism and supports whole-body circulation — acupuncture to improve digestive function, alongside herbs that promote fluid metabolism to help clear tissue congestion.

Pattern 04

Pelvic Inflammation Pattern

Seen alongside pelvic inflammatory conditions, or with heavy bleeding and odour. Ongoing inflammatory activity in pelvic tissue increases blood vessel permeability, and inflammatory byproducts mix with blood components to form thick, turbid tissue fluid. When this collects locally, it can obstruct tissue around the uterus and encourage fibroid formation.

Signs your body may be showing

Heavy periods, deep red in colour, sometimes with odour
Sticky, yellow discharge with odour
Vulvar itching
Fullness and burning pain in the lower abdomen
Bitter mouth, dry throat, dark urine

Treatment focus

Treatment calms inflammatory activity and helps clear stagnant waste from tissue — anti-inflammatory herbs alongside acupuncture to support pelvic circulation, with hemostatic herbs added when bleeding is heavy.

Pattern 05

Hormonal & Foundational Vitality Decline Pattern

Common after multiple pregnancies, repeated miscarriage, a naturally weaker constitution, or around menopause. When reproductive hormone secretion and overall metabolic strength decline, the driving force behind blood flow to the uterus weakens. This can show up as circulation slowing from fluid depletion — like a boat stranded when the water runs low — or as the metabolic "engine" behind circulation cooling down and flow grinding to a halt. Either way, when sustained, it leads to tissue stagnation that can develop into fibroids.

Signs your body may be showing

Very light periods, or conversely, heavy bleeding
Dull, persistent pulling pain in the lower abdomen
Aching, weak low back and knees, low energy
Reduced libido, frequent urination
Infertility or repeated miscarriage

Treatment focus

Treatment prioritizes restoring foundational strength and hormonal balance — herbs that build overall vitality, alongside acupuncture to support autonomic and endocrine regulation, gradually reviving circulation and resolving stagnant tissue.

How we think about treatment

  • 1

    The pattern matters. A stress-driven case and a cold, depleted case can call for very different approaches — what helps one can work against the other.

  • 2

    A fibroid isn't just a local growth. Circulation, metabolism, and hormonal balance all factor into the picture, and treatment works best when it addresses the pattern behind the fibroid, not the mass alone.

  • 3

    Overlap is common. Most patients show two or three patterns together rather than fitting neatly into one, which is why an in-person assessment is what actually determines your treatment plan.

Common questions

Can Traditional Eastern Medicine treatment make fibroids go away completely?
Response varies by individual depending on the fibroid's size, location, and number. Treatment doesn't aim to remove the fibroid directly, but rather improves the whole-body circulation and metabolic environment that allows fibroids to grow — helping ease symptoms and slow further growth. For larger fibroids or more severe symptoms, combining with conventional exams and treatment is the safer path.
How long does treatment take?
Generally at least 3 months of consistent treatment is needed, though the exact timeline varies with constitution and symptom severity.
How do I know which of the five patterns I am?
Most patients don't fit neatly into just one pattern — two or three often overlap. An in-person assessment and consultation is needed for an accurate read on your particular case.
Do I need both acupuncture and herbal medicine?
Not strictly required, but acupuncture is particularly effective for circulation and nervous system regulation, while herbal medicine supports metabolism and hormone balance — combining the two often works better. Your plan is adjusted to your individual condition.
Is there anything I can do to help prevent uterine fibroids?
Managing excess stress, avoiding cold exposure, regular exercise to support circulation, and a balanced diet can all help. If fibroids are already present, regular checkups to monitor size change are important.

This page is for general information only and isn't a substitute for individual diagnosis. If you're experiencing symptoms of uterine fibroids or have received this diagnosis, please speak with a qualified professional about your specific situation.
