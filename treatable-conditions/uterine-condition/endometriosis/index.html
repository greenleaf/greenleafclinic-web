Uterine Conditions

Endometriosis Isn't One Condition — It's a Circulation Pattern

The same diagnosis can look completely different from one person to the next. Traditional Eastern Medicine treatment starts by identifying what's actually driving the pain in your body, then builds a plan around it.

What's Actually Happening in Your Body

Endometriosis starts when tissue that normally lines the inside of the uterus ends up somewhere else in the pelvis. Every month, that displaced tissue still responds to your hormones — it builds up and breaks down just like it would inside the uterus. The problem is that it has nowhere to go. The breakdown irritates the surrounding tissue, and over time that repeated irritation leads to scarring and adhesions.

Traditional Eastern Medicine looks at this less as a single disease with one cause, and more as the downstream result of poor blood flow and circulation in the pelvis. What's different from person to person is why that circulation problem developed in the first place — stress and nervous system tension, chronic cold, whole-body depletion, active inflammation, or fluid metabolism issues can all lead to the same end result. That's why treatment doesn't start with the diagnosis — it starts with figuring out which of these is driving your case.

Five Patterns Behind the Pain

These patterns often overlap, and an accurate read usually needs an in-person exam. But most people recognize themselves in one or two of these right away.

Stress-Related Circulation Type Pattern A

Ongoing emotional stress or suppressed emotion keeps the nervous system in a tense, heightened state. That tension raises tightness in the blood vessels of the lower abdomen and pelvis, which restricts blood flow.

Bloating with sharp, stabbing pain around your period
Pain that shifts location rather than staying put
Chest tightness, breast fullness, frequent sighing
Clots in the menstrual blood; pain eases once a clot passes

Cold & Sluggish Circulation Type Pattern B

When the lower abdomen stays cold for long stretches, local blood vessels constrict and blood flow drops. Less oxygen and fewer nutrients reach the tissue, which lowers your pain threshold. Common with a lot of cold food, frequent cold exposure, or a naturally slower metabolism.

Severe cramping with a cold sensation in the lower abdomen
Pain noticeably eases with a heating pad or warm bath
Cold sensitivity, cold hands and feet, a cold low back
Light, dark-colored period with clots

Chronic Fatigue & Whole-Body Depletion Type Pattern C

Seen in people with a naturally lower baseline energy, or whose recovery capacity has been worn down by repeated pregnancy, childbirth, or surgery. As overall recovery and regulatory function decline, circulation and tissue repair in the pelvis weaken too — and over time that becomes chronic circulation dysfunction. Often seen alongside fertility difficulty.

Ongoing period pain with a heavy, sinking ache in the low back and tailbone
Light periods, or heavier periods with dark, watery blood
Dizziness, ringing in the ears, dull complexion, easy fatigue

Inflammatory Type Pattern D

Seen when pelvic inflammation is present, or when a diet heavy in fried food and alcohol raises internal "heat" — combined with tissue swelling and disrupted circulation. Closely related to ruptured (chocolate) cysts or active inflammatory changes.

Swollen, burning pain in the lower abdomen
Warmth makes the discomfort worse, not better
Heavy, dark period, sometimes with odor
Urinary discomfort, loose stool, flushed face and chest

Metabolic & Fluid Retention Type Pattern E

Weak digestive function means the body struggles to process fluid and metabolic waste. That retained fluid combines with poor circulation to create a dull, persistent ache in pelvic tissue. Often seen alongside weight gain tendencies or PCOS-type patterns.

Heavy, fixed-location lower abdominal discomfort
Period pain that's dull rather than sharp, and persistent
Swelling in hands, feet, or skin; light, thick period
Chest tightness and nausea

How Treatment Works

Treatment addresses your pattern from two directions at once — improving circulation directly, and treating whatever's driving the circulation problem in the first place.

Improving Circulation

Acupuncture widens peripheral blood vessels and increases blood flow to the pelvis, while calming an overactive nervous system to reduce the pain signal itself. When cold is a clear factor, heat therapy (moxibustion) is added to release local vessel constriction.

Addressing the Underlying Pattern

Herbal medicine targets whichever pattern applies to you — nervous system tension, sluggish circulation, whole-body depletion, inflammation, or fluid metabolism — so the circulation problem doesn't keep coming back. Approach often shifts with your cycle: more focus on circulation before your period, more focus on rebuilding recovery after.

Treatment length depends on severity and pattern. Inflammatory symptoms tend to respond in a relatively short time, while whole-body or chronic circulation issues usually take longer to shift.

Common Questions

Can treatment fully get rid of endometriosis?
The goal of Traditional Eastern Medicine treatment is to reduce pain, improve pelvic circulation and whole-body function, ease symptoms, and lower how often they recur — not to remove the lesion itself the way surgery does. How much someone responds varies from person to person. Please discuss diagnosis and treatment direction with your medical provider.
Can I combine this with conventional treatment, like hormone therapy or surgery?
Yes — many patients do both together. Let your practitioner know exactly what treatment you're currently receiving so your care can be coordinated safely alongside it.
Does acupuncture hurt?
Sensation varies by person, but most people feel a brief pinch followed by a relaxed feeling. For a sensitive area like the lower abdomen, your practitioner will walk you through what to expect before starting.
How long before I notice a difference from herbal medicine?
More acute, inflammatory symptoms can shift within a few weeks. Whole-body or chronic circulation issues usually need at least one full cycle — about a month — of consistent treatment before you can really evaluate how it's working.
How do I know which pattern applies to me?
In practice, these patterns often overlap. Getting an accurate read needs an in-person exam — pulse, tongue, and a detailed intake — so we'd recommend a consultation rather than trying to self-diagnose from a list.
