Making Sense of OCD, and How We Treat It

Washing your hands and needing to wash them again. Locking the door and going back to check anyway. If you live with OCD, you already know these loops don't make sense — and that knowing doesn't make them stop. Here's how we think about what's actually happening in the body, and how treatment works.

What's actually happening in the body

"I already washed my hands, but I feel like I need to wash them again." "I locked the door, but I went back to check anyway." People with OCD know this repetition doesn't make logical sense — and knowing that doesn't make it stop.

This isn't a matter of willpower. It's closer to a signal loop inside the nervous system — something's wrong → check → feel safe — that has become too sensitive. For most people, a single piece of information (the door is locked) is enough for the brain to register "done" and move on. When that off-switch doesn't fire cleanly, the brain keeps sending "not sure yet" signals, and the body repeats the same action to try to settle the unease.

What makes this circuit oversensitive is different from person to person. In the clinic, we see a handful of clearly distinct body patterns behind the same OCD diagnosis — and the right approach depends entirely on which one (or which combination) is driving your symptoms.

Four patterns we commonly see

Pattern 01An Always-On Alarm System

One of the most common patterns we see in clinic is a nervous system that's stuck in a chronically activated, stress-response state. Symptoms often trace back to a specific stressful event or a long stretch of sustained stress, and the checking behaviour rarely brings lasting relief.

Signs your body may be showing

Racing or pounding heart
Easily startled
Chronically tense muscles
Short fuse, quick to irritate

Treatment focus

The brain's "danger alarm" is already switched on most of the time, so even clear, reassuring information — the door really is locked — doesn't shut it off easily. The goal isn't to erase the intrusive thoughts themselves, but to lower the nervous system's overall tension and bring its sensitivity back down to a normal range. As muscle tension eases and the nervous system rebalances, both the checking behaviour and the intensity of the intrusive thoughts tend to settle together.

Pattern 02Gut-Brain Unrest

Another pattern we see often involves sluggish digestion. This isn't a coincidence — a growing body of research points to a close relationship between gut health and the brain's chemical signalling, often called the gut-brain connection.

Signs your body may be showing

Tightness in chest and upper abdomen
Frequent indigestion
Bloating
Low appetite

Treatment focus

When digestion stays unsettled for a long time, the signals reaching the brain become unsteady too, which can make it harder for the brain to register "this is certain" and move on. Treatment here focuses on restoring normal digestive function and easing the breath. As digestion settles and the chest tightness eases, the intensity of obsessive thinking and the underlying anxiety tend to improve in turn.

Pattern 03Depleted Reserves & Vascular Inflammation

When OCD and anxiety persist for a long time, the body can move past simple tension into something deeper: a gradual depletion of the fluids and tissue resources that keep it properly moisturized, alongside low-grade inflammation that can spread toward the blood vessels.

Signs your body may be showing

Recurring heat sensations
Dry mouth and throat
Dry skin
Shallow, unrefreshing sleep

Treatment focus

People at this stage usually notice more than just mental unease, and obsessive thoughts often intensify at night — this pattern tends to show up more in people who run lean and are naturally high-strung. Simply calming the nervous system isn't enough here; treatment needs to replenish the body's depleted fluid and tissue reserves while also addressing the inflammation that has reached the blood vessels.

Pattern 04A Naturally Sensitive Nervous System

Some people develop OCD symptoms without any clear trauma or stressful trigger — their nervous system has simply processed sensory input more intensely since birth, taking more time and energy to recover after everyday stimulation.

Signs your body may be showing

Startles easily, more fearful than average
Second-guesses decisions, checks and re-checks
Sensitive digestive system
Light sleep, vivid dreams

Treatment focus

The checking behaviour here isn't really driven by a sense of danger — it's more that sitting with uncertainty itself feels intolerable. This pattern overlaps with what psychology describes as the Highly Sensitive Person (HSP). Rather than simply calming excitability, treatment focuses on soothing an overloaded sensory-processing system while also supporting a sensitive digestive system. People with this pattern tend to be lean, wide-eyed, and finely built, and typically respond to treatment relatively quickly and gently.

How treatment unfolds

Rather than targeting the OCD symptoms first, the initial consultation looks at your body as a whole — digestion, sleep, muscle tension, body heat, and how you respond to stress — to identify which of the patterns above fits you most closely. Acupuncture and herbal medicine are then combined based on that picture, and change tends to follow this general order:

  1. Tension, digestion, and circulation stabilize first, matched to your specific pattern.
  2. Sleep quality improves, and overall anxiety begins to ease.
  3. Checking behaviours become less frequent and less intense, and intrusive thoughts start to pass through more easily rather than taking hold.

How long this takes depends on how severe and how long-standing your symptoms are, and on which pattern is at play — anywhere from a few weeks to several months.

Frequently asked questions

Can acupuncture or herbal medicine fully cure OCD?
It depends on the person. Lowering the body's overall tension and stabilizing the nervous system and digestion can clearly reduce how often and how strongly symptoms show up, but we can't promise complete resolution in every case. For severe or long-standing OCD, we recommend combining treatment with psychiatric care and counselling.
How do I know which of the four patterns applies to me?
It's best to find out through an initial consultation rather than guessing on your own. Most people show a mix of more than one pattern, so a proper intake and exam is needed to identify what's driving your symptoms most.
I'm currently taking psychiatric medication. Can I still receive treatment here?
Yes, the two can be used together. Please let us know exactly what you're taking and who is managing your care so we can plan your treatment safely.
How soon will I notice a difference?
Most people notice changes in sleep and digestion first, usually fairly early on. Changes in the OCD symptoms themselves tend to follow once overall body tension has come down.
Can children receive this treatment?
Yes — both acupuncture and herbal medicine can be adjusted in dose and approach for children and teens. An initial consultation is required first to confirm suitability.
