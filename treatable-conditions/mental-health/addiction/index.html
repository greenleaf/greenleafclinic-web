Addiction Isn't One Thing — Your Treatment Shouldn't Be Either

Traditional Eastern Medicine doesn't treat addiction as a single problem or a failure of willpower. It looks at what stage the body is in — and treats that stage.

Why "just quit" doesn't work

Addiction isn't simply a lack of willpower. In Traditional Eastern Medicine, it's understood as a state where the brain, nervous system, circulation, and energy metabolism are all pulled out of balance together. The same person can look completely different depending on how far the addiction has progressed — which is why the same generic advice rarely works for everyone.

Below are five patterns we commonly see, from the acute stage of active use through to long-term, chronic effects. Most people don't fit neatly into just one — but recognizing which pattern is closest to what you're experiencing right now is the first step toward the right kind of support.

Five patterns of addiction

Pattern 01

Acute Impulse Stage

This is addiction at its most intense. Cravings for the substance or behaviour — drugs, gambling, gaming — feel impossible to control, judgment becomes clouded, and it can spill over into impulsive or reckless behaviour.

Signs your body may be showing

Tight chest, heavy phlegm
Dry mouth, strong bad breath
Constipation
Trouble falling asleep, disturbing dreams

Treatment focus

This stage is essentially the brain's chemical messengers running into overdrive while whole-body inflammation piles on top — the reasoning part of the brain gets drowned out by the noise. The priority is calming that overexcited nervous system and clearing the mind, settling the surge before building toward the next stage.

Pattern 02

Withdrawal Stage

This shows up after a period of active addiction, once the substance or behaviour stops. Both body and mind are running on empty, and that depletion shows up as intense anxiety.

Signs your body may be showing

Restlessness and anxiety without the substance
Tingling hands and feet, heart palpitations
Hot palms and soles, cold sweats
Dizziness, ringing in the ears
Achy lower back and knees
Strong urges with no energy to act on them

Treatment focus

This is the autonomic nervous system and hormonal regulation running on fumes. The body's stress-response system is so depleted that it overreacts to small triggers while having nothing left to recover with. The priority here is rebuilding the body's core reserves and calming an oversensitive nervous system — slowly, not by pushing hard.

Pattern 03

Depleted / Low-Energy Type

Here the person is worn down physically and mentally, and turns to the addictive behaviour to numb emptiness and exhaustion, even briefly. Willpower itself often feels diminished.

Signs your body may be showing

Easily discouraged, low motivation
Dull complexion, no vitality
Heart palpitations, forgetfulness
Poor appetite
Loose stools
Sleeps a lot, still tired all the time

Treatment focus

This pattern reflects a broad slowdown in energy production and in how the body generates its own mood-supporting chemistry. Because the body's basic capacity to generate vitality is running low, the addictive behaviour becomes a stand-in for missing energy and emotional fullness. The priority is rebuilding overall energy production while creating a more stable emotional foundation.

Pattern 04

Agitated / Aggressive Type

Seen more often with stimulant-type substances or highly arousing behaviours, this pattern combines an intense pursuit of pleasure with a marked edge of aggression.

Signs your body may be showing

Flushed face, bloodshot eyes
Sudden irritability, talks more than usual
Explosive reactions to small things
Intense anger if the behaviour is blocked
Bitter taste in mouth, constipation
Frequent insomnia and headaches

Treatment focus

This pattern points to an overactive stress-response system combined with whole-body inflammation — essentially the body stuck in "fight" mode. The priority is firmly calming that overdriven nervous system response and rebuilding the stable footing needed to manage impulses.

Pattern 05

Chronic Impairment Type

This is the long-term, chronic stage — the result of drug or alcohol addiction over an extended period, where the brain and nervous system have accumulated real, lasting effects.

Signs your body may be showing

Noticeably worse memory and focus
Emotionally flat, more stubborn
Dull, greyish complexion
Headaches in the same spot each time
Slower speech and movement
Slow to bounce back, even after withdrawal

Treatment focus

This pattern reflects reduced blood flow to the brain over time and the tissue changes that build up as a result — the brain has been running short on oxygen and nutrients for a long stretch, and cognitive function shows it. The priority is improving circulation throughout the body, including to the brain, to support as much recovery as the body is capable of.

How we think about treatment

  • 1

    The stage matters. "Addiction" isn't one condition — what the body needs in the acute stage, during withdrawal, and in the long-term chronic stage are three different things.

  • 2

    The pattern matters. An agitated, overactive pattern and a depleted, low-energy pattern can call for opposite approaches — what helps one can work against the other.

  • 3

    We look at the whole body. Addiction isn't only a brain problem. It involves the nervous system, circulation, and energy metabolism together, and treatment works best when it addresses all of them.

Common questions

Can Traditional Eastern Medicine treat addiction on its own?
Addiction is a complex issue shaped by psychological and social factors as well as physical ones. Our approach works to restore the body's physiological balance to support recovery, and tends to be most effective alongside counselling or other professional treatment rather than as a stand-alone solution.
Can acupuncture or herbal medicine ease withdrawal symptoms?
It varies from person to person, but approaches that stabilize the nervous system and rebuild the body's core energy can help ease the anxiety, sleep problems, and other symptoms common during withdrawal.
How long does treatment typically take?
This depends heavily on how long the addiction has gone on, which pattern it falls into, and each person's own capacity to recover. The chronic impairment pattern in particular tends to need a longer recovery timeline.
Is the approach different for drugs, alcohol, gaming, or gambling?
The object of the addiction differs, but we tailor treatment to the pattern the body is showing — agitated, depleted, chronic, and so on — rather than to the specific substance or behaviour. An accurate assessment starts with a proper consultation.
How can I support a family member dealing with addiction?
The first step is working with a professional to understand which stage and pattern they're closest to. Recovery has its own pace for each stage — pushing too hard, too fast tends to backfire, so patience matched to that pace matters.

This page is for general information only and isn't a substitute for individual diagnosis. If addiction is affecting you or someone close to you, please speak with a qualified professional about your specific situation.
