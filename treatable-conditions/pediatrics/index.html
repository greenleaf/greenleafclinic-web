Pediatrics

A Child's Body Isn't a Small Adult's Body

Digestion, immunity, and temperature regulation are all still under construction in a growing child — which is exactly why colds, stomachaches, restless sleep, and slow-to-settle nervous systems show up so often, and change so quickly. We treat with that in mind, using gentle methods matched to your child's age, weight, and constitution.

Why Children's Bodies Work Differently

A child isn't a scaled-down adult. Digestion, immunity, the autonomic nervous system, and temperature regulation are all systems still under construction — and each one behaves a little differently because of it.

The digestive tract's muscle movement and enzyme production are still maturing, so the same meal can sit heavier on a child's stomach than an adult's, and the gut lining reacts more easily to irritation. The immune system is still building its library of past infections, which is why colds, ear infections, and tonsillitis tend to show up again and again in early childhood — not because a child is unusually unwell, but because that repeated exposure is how immune memory gets built.

Metabolism runs hot to fuel growth, while the nervous system's temperature control and fluid balance haven't fully caught up — so fevers can climb quickly with even a minor infection, and an overexcited nervous system can occasionally show up as a febrile seizure. Day to day, this same unsettled autonomic system can also show up as being easily startled or quick to fuss.

All of this adds up to symptoms that move fast. A mild sniffle can turn into a high fever within hours; a chest cold can worsen quickly. But the same quality that makes symptoms change quickly also works in a child's favour — cell turnover and recovery capacity are high during these years. Our goal with pediatric care is to support that natural recovery ability so the body can find its own balance more quickly.

Common Patterns We Treat

Pattern 01

Underdeveloped Respiratory Defenses

The upper airway's immune defenses are still building up their response to everyday viruses and irritants, so runny noses, coughs, and throat inflammation tend to recur. This often becomes more noticeable during seasonal transitions or shortly after starting daycare or school, when exposure to new viruses jumps.

Signs your child may be showing

Frequent runny nose or congestion
Recurring ear infections
Sore throat or swollen tonsils
Catches colds more often than peers
Slow to bounce back after a cold
Symptoms flare with season changes
Treatment focus

We work to strengthen the upper airway's mucosal defenses and shorten recovery time between illnesses, pairing gentle acupuncture with herbal formulas chosen to support circulation and immune function in the throat, nose, and ears.

Pattern 02

Weak Digestive Absorption

Gut motility and enzyme production are still maturing, so it doesn't take much — a rich meal, something cold, a stretch of stress — before the stomach or bowels react. Some children are especially sensitive to cold foods, greasy foods, or dairy, and a persistently low appetite can quietly affect growth over time.

Signs your child may be showing

Stomachaches after eating
Irregular bowel patterns
Picky eating or low appetite
Sensitivity to certain foods
Low energy after meals
Slow weight gain
Treatment focus

We focus on restoring smoother gut motility and absorption, with an eye on how digestion connects to overall energy and growth over the longer term — not just settling today's stomachache.

Pattern 03

A Nervous System Still Finding Its Footing

The autonomic nervous system and sensory regulation are still stabilizing, so external stimulation can produce an outsized reaction — a sudden noise, a change in routine, overtiredness. This can show up as repetitive movements, trouble holding focus, or frequent waking through the night.

Signs your child may be showing

Repetitive blinking, throat-clearing, or similar movements
Trouble focusing or sitting still
Frequent waking or crying at night
Startles easily
Unusually fussy or irritable
Restless, easily interrupted sleep
Treatment focus

We work to calm an overactive nervous system and support steadier autonomic regulation, looking at sleep and emotional wellbeing alongside the primary symptom rather than in isolation.

Pattern 04

A Foundation for Growth That Needs Support

Whole-body metabolism and hormone balance are still settling into place, and some children tire more easily or grow more slowly than their peers as a result. An immature bladder-control system can also play a role in bedwetting that continues past the age most children outgrow it.

Signs your child may be showing

Tires more easily than other children
Grows more slowly than peers
Still wets the bed at age 5 or older
Low stamina during activity
Smaller appetite than expected for age
Catches minor illnesses often
Treatment focus

We support whole-body circulation and metabolism, working to create the internal conditions for a child's body to reach its own natural growth potential — a longer-term, constitutional approach rather than a quick fix.

Pattern 05

An Overreactive Skin & Immune Response

The skin barrier and immune regulation are still developing, so external irritants can trigger a stronger inflammatory reaction than they would in an adult. These flares are often connected to digestion and sleep, and rarely respond to skin care alone.

Signs your child may be showing

Dry, easily irritated skin
Recurring rashes or hives
Frequent scratching, especially at night
Flares tied to certain foods or seasons
Sleep disrupted by itching
Redness or roughness in skin folds
Treatment focus

We support skin barrier recovery while working to calm the body's overall inflammatory response, taking digestion and sleep quality into account as part of the same picture.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is herbal medicine safe for children?

Yes. Formulas are adjusted for your child's weight, age, and constitution — this isn't simply a smaller adult dose, but a gentler formulation suited to a child's still-developing digestion.

My child is afraid of needles. Can they still be treated?

Pediatric acupuncture uses extremely fine, gentle needles, and non-insertion techniques are available for children who are anxious about needles. We adjust the intensity of treatment based on how your child responds.

My child catches colds often — can this help?

Frequent colds, ear infections, and tonsillitis often point to underdeveloped respiratory defenses. Between illnesses, constitutional treatment can help reduce how often and how severely these infections recur.

Can this support my child's growth?

We take a whole-body approach — supporting circulation, digestion, and sleep together — to help create the conditions for your child's body to reach its own natural growth potential. It's a longer-term, constitutional approach rather than a quick fix.

Can tic disorders or ADHD be treated?

We work toward restoring balance in the nervous system, taking related symptoms like sleep, digestion, and emotional wellbeing into account together. For symptoms that are significantly affecting school or daily life, we recommend working alongside your child's medical team.

At what age can bedwetting be treated?

Treatment is generally considered once bedwetting continues past age 5. We look at bladder control alongside overall metabolic development rather than treating it in isolation.

At what age can my child start treatment here?

There's no strict minimum age — even infants can be treated using gentle, non-insertion techniques. Dosing and treatment intensity are always adjusted for your child's age, weight, and constitution.
