Acne That Keeps Coming Back Usually Has a Pattern Behind It

Breakouts on the face, chest, or back can come from several different underlying imbalances — not just "oily skin." Traditional Eastern Medicine looks at which pattern is driving your specific case, so treatment addresses the cause, not just the surface.

Why the Same Breakout Doesn't Respond to the Same Treatment

Acne shows up on the face, chest, and back as blackheads, whiteheads, red papules, pus-filled pimples, and — in more stubborn cases — hard nodules or cysts under the skin. Most conventional approaches treat all of these the same way: dry it out, kill the bacteria, control the oil. But two people with what looks like the same breakout can have very different things driving it underneath.

In Traditional Eastern Medicine, acne is understood as a build-up of internal heat and dampness that surfaces through the skin. Diet, stress, hormones, and digestion all feed into that build-up differently from person to person, which is why we start by identifying which pattern is actually driving your skin — not by assuming every case needs the same protocol.

The Five Patterns Behind Acne

Below are the five patterns we most commonly see in clinic. Most people recognize themselves in one or two — it's rarely just one in isolation, especially in longer-standing cases.

Pattern 01

Surface Inflammation Pattern

This is usually the earliest and mildest stage — small blackheads, whiteheads, and light red bumps concentrated around the forehead, nose, and cheeks. It tends to show up when the upper body is reacting to external irritation, often alongside a cold, seasonal change, or general dryness rather than a deeper internal imbalance.

Signs your body may be showing

Small, scattered blackheads and whiteheads
Mild redness with occasional itching or tenderness
Skin feels dry rather than oily
Occasional sore throat or stuffy nose alongside breakouts

Treatment focus

Care is aimed at clearing surface inflammation and calming the upper body's reactivity, using lighter acupuncture points and herbal formulas suited to early-stage, external presentations.

Pattern 02

Digestive Heat and Excess Oil Pattern

The most common pattern we see in clinic. Rich, fried, sweet, or dairy-heavy eating along with alcohol can build up heat and dampness in the digestive system, which then surfaces on the skin as very oily, inflamed breakouts. This pattern tends to produce the classic painful red pimples that come to a head with visible pus.

Signs your body may be showing

Noticeably oily skin, especially nose, forehead, and cheeks
Painful red bumps that develop yellow pus
Bad breath, sticky stool, or occasional constipation
Bloating, low appetite, or frequent thirst

Treatment focus

Treatment focuses on clearing digestive heat and dampness and supporting healthier digestion overall, so the skin has less internal heat to process in the first place.

Pattern 03

Severe Inflammatory Pattern

When heat and dampness build up over time without resolving, they can intensify into a more severe, deeply inflamed pattern. This shows up as large, firm, painful nodules and cysts on the face, chest, or back — sometimes merging into connected patches — with a real risk of scarring if left unaddressed.

Signs your body may be showing

Large, hard, painful nodules or cysts
Lesions that merge into larger inflamed patches
Noticeable scarring after breakouts heal
General thirst, constipation, or feeling overheated

Treatment focus

This pattern calls for stronger clearing and cooling strategies aimed at reducing the intensity of inflammation and lowering the risk of further scarring, alongside close monitoring of progress.

Pattern 04

Hormonal Cycle Pattern

Common in women, this pattern tracks closely with the menstrual cycle — breakouts flare in the week or so before a period and ease up afterward. It tends to concentrate along the jawline, chin, and lower cheeks, and often comes with other signs that the cycle itself is out of balance.

Signs your body may be showing

Breakouts that flare before your period and settle after
Concentrated along the jaw, chin, and lower cheeks
Irregular cycles, cramping, or breast tenderness
Increased irritability or mood changes before your period

Treatment focus

Treatment centers on regulating the menstrual cycle and easing the hormonal fluctuations behind these breakouts, with acupuncture and herbal medicine timed to your cycle where useful.

Pattern 05

Deep Cystic and Scarring Pattern

In longer-standing cases, unresolved heat can thicken into stagnant fluid and blood, producing firm cysts and nodules that sit deep under the skin. These lesions tend to be dark red or purplish, aren't especially painful, are slow to come to a head, and often leave lasting pigmentation or raised scarring behind.

Signs your body may be showing

Firm cysts or nodules that persist for weeks
Dark red or purplish discoloration of lesions
Lingering dark marks or raised scars after healing
Little pain despite the visible size of the lesion

Treatment focus

Because this pattern is more entrenched, care focuses on improving circulation and gradually breaking up stagnation, which typically requires a longer, more consistent course of treatment.

What Treatment Looks Like

After identifying which pattern (or combination of patterns) fits your case, treatment typically combines acupuncture with a customized herbal formula, along with practical guidance on diet and lifestyle factors that tend to feed each pattern. The goal is to reduce active breakouts and lower how often they recur — not to promise a permanent cure, since acne can have multiple contributing causes over a lifetime.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does acne treatment usually take?

It depends on the pattern and how long the acne has been present. Surface-level, early-stage acne often responds within a few weeks, while deeper cystic or hormonally-driven patterns generally need a longer, more consistent course of treatment. We'll give you a realistic timeline once we've assessed your specific case.

Can acupuncture help with acne if I'm already using topical or prescription treatments?

Yes — acupuncture and herbal medicine are generally compatible alongside topical treatments or prescriptions from your dermatologist. Let us know what you're currently using so we can plan treatment appropriately.

Will diet changes actually make a difference?

For the digestive heat and damp-heat patterns especially, diet plays a significant role. We'll go over which foods tend to aggravate your specific pattern rather than giving generic "avoid sugar and dairy" advice that may not apply to your case.

Can this help with acne scarring, not just active breakouts?

Treatment is primarily aimed at reducing active inflammation and recurrence, which in turn reduces the chance of new scarring forming. For existing scars, we can discuss whether facial acupuncture may be a helpful complement — feel free to ask us at your consultation.
