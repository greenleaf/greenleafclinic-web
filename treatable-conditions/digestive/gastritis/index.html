Digestive Problems

Gastritis Isn't One Thing — Your Stomach Has a Pattern

"My stomach burns," "I can't digest anything," "my chest feels tight after eating" — everyone describes gastritis differently, because it isn't one condition. Traditional Eastern Medicine looks at how your stomach is actually reacting, then treats that pattern specifically.

Why "gastritis" isn't one thing

"My stomach won't digest anything," "I feel a burning ache under my ribs," "my chest tightens after I eat" — these are all common ways people describe gastritis, but they don't point to the same problem. Some people flare up the moment they eat anything cold. Others feel fine after eating and worse on an empty stomach. Some barely notice hunger at all.

In Traditional Eastern Medicine, we don't treat gastritis as a single diagnosis. We look at how your stomach is actually behaving right now — what sets it off, what calms it down, and what that pattern tells us about what's happening underneath — and build the treatment around that.

What your stomach needs to work properly

Your stomach breaks food down and moves it along in a constant cycle. That cycle depends on two things working together: enough blood flow to the stomach lining to keep it protected and able to repair itself, and steady, forward-moving muscle contractions so food doesn't sit too long or back up.

When either one is disrupted, you get inflammation, pain, burning, or bloating. Over years of clinical observation, we've come to recognize a handful of recurring patterns in how that disruption shows up. Below are the seven we see most often.

The patterns we look for

Pattern 01

Cold Spasm

Sudden, sharp pain under the ribs after a big cold meal or a blast of cold wind — often with cold hands and feet, chills, and sometimes watery vomiting or diarrhea. Cold causes the blood vessels feeding the stomach to constrict, cutting local circulation right when the stomach muscle is reacting with a spasm. Warmth reopens those vessels and the pain eases; more cold makes it worse.

Signs your body may be showing

Sudden, sharp pain after cold food or cold exposure
Cold hands, feet, and a chilled feeling
Watery vomiting or loose stool in severe episodes
Warmth brings quick relief; cold makes it worse
Treatment focus

Acupuncture and herbal support aimed at reopening local circulation and easing the muscle spasm, so the acute reaction settles quickly.

Pattern 02

Overload

A tight, bloated fullness under the ribs after overeating, a big binge, or rich food — tender to the touch, with sour burping, bad breath, and nausea that actually feels better after vomiting. When more food arrives than the stomach can process, it sits and ferments, producing gas and pushing acid back up.

Signs your body may be showing

Tight, tender fullness after overeating
Sour burping and bad breath
Nausea that eases after vomiting
Symptoms tied clearly to a specific meal
Treatment focus

The priority is getting the stomach moving again, so what's sitting there clears out and stops fermenting.

Pattern 03

Stress Response

The classic "nervous stomach" — a tight, wandering discomfort under the ribs and along the sides that moves around and flares the moment stress hits, along with frequent burping, a lump-in-the-throat feeling, and a lot of sighing. Ongoing stress shifts your nervous system into a state where blood flow gets redirected away from digestion, so stomach movement and secretions both slow down — even when a scope shows no visible damage.

Signs your body may be showing

Discomfort that moves around and flares with stress
Frequent burping or a lump-in-the-throat feeling
A lot of sighing
Symptoms persist even with a clear endoscopy
Treatment focus

We work to bring the nervous system back into balance so blood flow and normal movement return to the stomach — treating the stress response alongside the stomach itself, not the stomach alone.

Pattern 04

Acid & Erosion

A burning, searing pain under the ribs, often with a dry, bitter mouth, strong bad breath, and a heavy feeling in the body — common after repeated alcohol, spicy or greasy food, or smoking. Acid and digestive enzymes are running high while the protective mucus layer can't keep up, so the lining itself gets irritated, sometimes with visible redness or swelling.

Signs your body may be showing

Burning, searing pain under the ribs
Dry, bitter mouth and strong bad breath
A heavy, sluggish feeling in the body
History of alcohol, spicy food, or smoking
Treatment focus

The aim is to calm the overactive secretions and cool the irritation in the lining itself.

Pattern 05

Vascular Damage

Fixed, needle-like pain in one exact spot, worse at night, with a strong aversion to being touched or pressed there — sometimes with a history of bleeding. Long-standing inflammation can damage the small blood vessels in the stomach wall, leading to poor local circulation and occasional micro-bleeding, which is why the pain sits precisely in one place rather than moving around. If there's any sign of bleeding — vomiting blood or dark, tarry stool — this needs to be assessed by a medical doctor alongside any other care.

Signs your body may be showing

Sharp, fixed pain in one exact spot
Worse at night
Very sensitive to pressure on that spot
History of gastric bleeding or ulcer
Treatment focus

Support for healing the damaged vessel tissue and improving local circulation, done alongside your medical follow-up rather than in place of it.

Pattern 06

Low Circulation

A dull, low-grade ache that's fairly constant, but eases with warmth or after eating and worsens on an empty stomach — often paired with ongoing fatigue, cold hands and feet, loose stool, and cold food triggering diarrhea. This is the most common pattern in long-standing gastritis: blood flow to the digestive tract has been running low for a while, so digestion overall works at a reduced capacity and reacts poorly to cold or hunger.

Signs your body may be showing

Dull, ongoing ache that eases with warmth or food
Worse on an empty stomach
Ongoing fatigue and cold hands and feet
Loose stool, especially after cold food
Treatment focus

We focus on rebuilding baseline circulation and digestive capacity, so the stomach becomes less reactive to cold and hunger over time.

Pattern 07

Mucosal Depletion

A burning or gnawing discomfort, but with little to no appetite and poor hunger cues, along with a dry mouth and throat that's worse at night, warmth in the palms, soles, and chest, and dry stool. Commonly seen alongside atrophic gastritis. Long-term depletion leaves the lining without the fluids it needs to regenerate — it thins and dries out, and the body produces a relative, low-grade heat sensation even though there's no active inflammation.

Signs your body may be showing

Burning discomfort with little to no appetite
Dry mouth and throat, worse at night
Warmth in the palms, soles, and chest
Dry stool
Treatment focus

Treatment aims to replenish the lining and support gradual regeneration, paired with regular medical monitoring since atrophic changes need to be tracked over time.

How treatment is approached

Rather than treating "gastritis" as one fixed label, we start by identifying which of the patterns above best matches how your body is actually reacting right now. Whether it's cold exposure, stress, excess acid, or long-term low circulation changes which acupuncture points and which herbal support make sense — the approach for a stress-driven flare looks nothing like the approach for a cold-spasm episode.

Acupuncture is generally used to improve local circulation to the digestive tract and calm an overactive nervous system, helping stomach movement return to normal. Herbal support is adjusted by pattern — calming inflammation, restoring healthy movement, or supporting the lining's ability to regenerate, depending on what's needed. Where stress is a significant driver, we also address the nervous system's overall state, not just the stomach.

Common questions

My endoscopy came back clear, but I still can't digest properly. Can this still be helped?

Yes — this is very common. Ongoing nervous system stress can slow stomach movement and secretions even when there's no visible damage on a scope. This kind of functional digestive trouble tends to respond well to a Traditional Eastern Medicine approach.

Does acupuncture actually help with gastritis?

Acupuncture is generally used to improve circulation to the digestive tract and ease nervous system tension, which supports more normal stomach movement. It tends to be most helpful for stress-related and functional digestive symptoms.

Is treatment different for acute versus long-standing gastritis?

Yes. An acute flare — usually from cold exposure or overeating — is approached by resolving that specific trigger quickly. Long-standing gastritis is approached more gradually, focused on rebuilding circulation and the stomach's capacity to regenerate, and generally takes longer to see change.

How can I tell if mine is more "excess acid" or more "low circulation"?

As a rough guide: burning discomfort, a bitter mouth, and symptoms that worsen with spicy or rich food tend to point toward excess acid and irritation. Symptoms that ease with warmth or food and worsen on an empty stomach tend to point toward low circulation. The two often overlap, so an accurate read usually needs an in-person assessment.

I've been diagnosed with atrophic gastritis — is this still something you can help manage?

Yes. Atrophic gastritis needs regular endoscopic monitoring, and we'd recommend continuing that alongside any care here. Traditional Eastern Medicine can be a helpful part of managing symptoms and supporting overall recovery alongside your medical follow-up.
