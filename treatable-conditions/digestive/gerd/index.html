Digestive Problems

GERD (Acid Reflux) Treatment

Burning chest pain, sour regurgitation, that uncomfortable fullness after meals — reflux isn't always simply "too much stomach acid." The same symptoms can come from several different underlying states, and figuring out which one is driving yours is where treatment starts.

Why does reflux happen?

A ring of muscle between your stomach and esophagus — the lower esophageal sphincter — normally keeps stomach contents where they belong. How well it holds, and how quickly your stomach empties, are both regulated by your autonomic nervous system. Stress, irregular meals, rich or spicy food, and chronic fatigue can throw that system off balance, loosening the sphincter or slowing digestion, and that's when reflux shows up.

Traditional Eastern Medicine doesn't treat every case of reflux the same way. We look at what's driving your body into this state, because different patterns call for different approaches.

Six patterns behind GERD

The same symptom — reflux — shows up differently depending on what's behind it. Here's how we tell them apart.

PATTERN 01

Stress & Tension

Ongoing mental strain keeps your sympathetic nervous system switched on, which directly affects digestive rhythm and sphincter function. Symptoms often flare together with emotional stress.

Signs your body may be showing

Heartburn and sour regurgitation
Frequent burping
Tightness in chest or upper abdomen
Lump-in-throat feeling
Symptoms track with mood
Sleep disturbance
Treatment focus

Calming an overactive stress response and restoring nervous-system balance. Acupuncture releases tension held in the chest and abdomen, paired with support for sleep quality — which often speeds recovery along.

PATTERN 02

Mucosal Irritation

Repeated alcohol, spicy food, or fatty food inflames the stomach lining and pushes acid output up, leaving the lining more reactive to everything else.

Signs your body may be showing

Intense heartburn and reflux
Sour burping, bitter or sour taste
Thirst and bad breath
Gum swelling
Constipation
Burning in the upper abdomen
Treatment focus

Calming inflammation and easing constipation to reduce the overall load on the digestive system. Most effective alongside cutting back on alcohol and spicy food.

PATTERN 03

Weak Digestion & Poor Circulation

A naturally weaker digestive system, or long-term fatigue, leaves gut motility and circulation sluggish — this pattern is especially sensitive to cold.

Signs your body may be showing

Milder reflux and heartburn
Dull discomfort, eased by warmth
Worse after cold food or drinks
Chronic fatigue, poor appetite
Loose stools
Cold hands and feet
Treatment focus

Warming the system and boosting circulation and metabolism. Acupuncture paired with moxibustion or heat therapy gradually rebuilds digestive motility — avoiding cold food and keeping regular mealtimes matters a lot here.

PATTERN 04

Reduced Mucosal Repair

Chronic depletion, overwork, or ongoing exposure to irritating foods leaves the stomach lining unable to hold moisture or repair itself well.

Signs your body may be showing

Burning pain without much hunger sensation
Reduced appetite
Dry mouth or throat, worse at night
Dry retching or hiccups
Dry, scant stools
Warm palms and soles
Treatment focus

Restoring the lining's moisture and repair capacity through nourishing therapy that strengthens the stomach's defenses. Recovery tends to be gradual here, so consistency matters.

PATTERN 05

Delayed Stomach Emptying

Overeating, binge eating, or irregular meals leave food sitting in the stomach too long, fermenting and affecting the smell of reflux and burping.

Signs your body may be showing

Sour or rotten-smelling burps
Sour regurgitation
Fullness and blockage in the upper abdomen
Relief after vomiting
Aversion to food smells
Constipation or incomplete bowel movements
Treatment focus

Clearing stagnation and getting digestion moving again, paired with adjusting meal size and timing. This pattern tends to respond fastest once habits change.

PATTERN 06

Chronic Inflammation & Tissue Change

Long-standing, repeated symptoms that have led to lasting changes in the esophageal lining. This pattern needs careful attention and should always be confirmed with a GI workup first.

Signs your body may be showing

Persistent pain behind the breastbone
Strong lump-in-throat sensation
Difficulty swallowing
Fixed burning pain in one spot
Recurring nausea and vomiting
Treatment focus

Supporting circulation and tissue recovery as a complementary measure — only after structural issues (strictures, Barrett's esophagus, and similar) have been ruled out by a gastroenterologist.

If you're experiencing difficulty swallowing or unexplained weight loss, please see a gastroenterologist for evaluation before or alongside treatment here.

How treatment works

Rather than only suppressing acid, treatment aims to bring your autonomic nervous system and digestive rhythm back into balance. Acupuncture helps regulate gut motility and nervous-system balance, and herbal support is matched to your specific pattern — protecting the lining, improving circulation, or easing tension, as needed. Because the same symptom can come from different causes, your first visit includes a full picture of your digestion, sleep, temperature sensitivity, and bowel habits.

Frequently asked questions

Is GERD related to stress?
Often, yes. Stress affects the lower esophageal sphincter and stomach motility through the autonomic nervous system, so symptoms frequently worsen alongside emotional strain.
Can I continue conventional treatment alongside this?
Yes. If you're already taking acid-suppressing medication, let us know so we can plan treatment around it.
How long does treatment take?
It varies by pattern and how long symptoms have been present. Recent, stress-related cases often improve within a few weeks, while long-standing, tissue-change cases take longer.
When should I see a doctor first?
Always get checked first if you have difficulty swallowing, unexplained weight loss, fixed pain, or black stools or vomiting blood. Our care is designed to help once those risk signs have been ruled out.
Do I need to adjust my diet too?
Yes. Late meals, overeating, spicy food, and alcohol can worsen symptoms regardless of pattern, so we usually address habits alongside treatment.
