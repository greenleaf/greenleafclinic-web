Fertility & Women's Health

Comfortable, Supported Care Through Every Stage of Pregnancy

Pregnancy brings the biggest physical changes a body goes through — shifting hormones, an expanding uterus, more blood volume — which naturally bring along nausea, back pain, swelling, and sleepless nights. Acupuncture is one of the few treatments that can safely work with these changes, week by week, alongside your OB's care.

Common Discomforts, Stage by Stage

Almost every pregnancy comes with some combination of these — none of them mean anything is wrong, they're just what happens as your body makes room for a baby.

Morning sickness (nausea & vomiting)
Low back pain & sciatica
Swelling in hands & feet
Trouble sleeping
Baby in breech position
Preparing for labor

Morning Sickness

Nausea and vomiting in early pregnancy happen because hormonal shifts temporarily slow down the movement of the digestive tract. Acupuncture works by calming and rebalancing the nervous system that controls that movement, helping digestion return toward normal — without medication, which makes it a treatment many people prefer in the first trimester.

75%+of patients reported noticeably less nausea within days of starting treatment
2 wkstypical course of care used in the supporting clinical study

Based on a clinical study of more than 30 pregnant patients treated with acupuncture over a two-week period.

Low Back Pain & Sciatica

As pregnancy progresses, added weight and a forward-shifting center of gravity put extra load on the lower back and pelvis. The hormone relaxin, released to loosen the pelvis for delivery, also loosens joints and ligaments more broadly — which is often what triggers back pain or sciatica alongside it. Acupuncture relaxes tight muscles and improves local circulation to ease the pain, and it's a particularly useful option during pregnancy since pain medication is limited.

Swelling (Edema)

In later pregnancy, the growing uterus presses on the vein that returns blood from the legs, which slows circulation and leads to swelling in the hands and feet. Acupuncture can help move that stagnant fluid along, easing the swelling — and it can be applied to wrist swelling as well, which sometimes progresses into carpal tunnel–related numbness or pain.

Trouble Sleeping

A growing belly, frequent trips to the bathroom, and hormonal changes all make it harder to fall — and stay — asleep during pregnancy. Acupuncture calms an overactive nervous system so the body can settle into rest more easily.

Bleeding or Reduced Fetal Movement

Some pregnancies go through unsettling stretches of occasional spotting or a baby who seems less active than usual. In these situations, acupuncture and herbal medicine can play a supportive role — helping circulation to the uterus and the mother's overall condition.

This is never a substitute for your OB's care. Regular ultrasounds and monitoring from your obstetrician are essential during this time, and acupuncture is used alongside that care — not in place of it.

Breech Presentation

It's common for a baby to be positioned breech (bottom-down) at some point in pregnancy, and most — 96 to 97% — turn head-down on their own as full term approaches. If a baby stays breech, your OB may offer an External Cephalic Version (ECV), a procedure that manually repositions the baby through the abdomen.

Moxibustion is a traditional method used alongside this, to encourage the baby to turn on its own. Gentle heat is applied for 10–15 minutes a day, over 7–14 days, to a point at the outer corner of the little toe (Bladder 67 / Zhiyin) — the added stimulation is thought to increase fetal movement and prompt the baby to reposition.

Labor Preparation Acupuncture

Starting around 36–37 weeks, weekly acupuncture (typically 2–3 sessions per week) helps prepare both mother and baby for a smoother delivery. Treatment uses 4–5 points on the arms and legs, chosen based on each patient's constitution and condition — abdominal points are never used during pregnancy.

Physically, it helps soften the cervix, supports the baby into position, builds the mother's stamina, and continues to manage back pain, poor sleep, and swelling. Just as importantly, it gives mothers a sense of steady preparation heading into labor.

For mothers who pass their due date without labor starting, acupuncture can also offer a gentle, natural nudge. In a study of 45 patients at term, those treated with acupuncture went into labor in an average of 5 days from their due date, versus 7.9 days for those who weren't — and needed induction 20% of the time, versus 35%.

A Note on Safety

The information above is general and educational — how you respond to treatment, and what's appropriate for you, depends on your individual pregnancy. Treatment during pregnancy is always meant to complement your OB's regular checkups, never replace them, and if you notice bleeding, sudden pain, or anything that feels wrong, please contact your doctor right away. If you're dealing with any of the symptoms above, we're happy to talk it through.
