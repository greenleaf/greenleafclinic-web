ENT & Eye

Ear, Nose, Throat & Eye Symptom Relief

The ear, nose, throat and eyes all sit at the surface of the body, in direct contact with the outside world. Thin membranes and fine blood vessels make them sensitive to small shifts in circulation and inflammation — so the same symptom, like tinnitus or a stuffy nose, can come from different underlying causes and call for different approaches.

How We Approach These Symptoms

Traditional Eastern Medicine treatment for the ear, nose, throat and eyes doesn't start from a diagnosis label — it starts by identifying what's actually happening in the tissue right now: Is blood flow to the area reduced? Is there active inflammation and swelling? Is fluid pooling? Is the membrane dry? The same symptom, like "tinnitus" or "nasal congestion," can point to different underlying processes, and treatment follows the process rather than the symptom name alone.

Ear

Tinnitus, hearing loss, ear fullness, dizziness, ear infections, ear pain. The ear needs a steady, uninterrupted blood supply to maintain fine hearing function, while the eustachian tube and inner ear are narrow passages where even a small shift in fluid circulation affects pressure and hearing signals.

Pattern 01 Reduced Local Blood Flow

When blood supply reaching the inner ear drops, it often shows up as a constant, low-pitched ringing and a hearing loss that develops gradually. This pattern is common in people who are overworked, sleep-deprived, or chronically fatigued.

Treatment focus

We work on improving whole-body circulation and microcirculation to the ear.

Pattern 02 Fluid Retention

When fluid pools in the narrow spaces of the ear, it can cause a feeling of fullness, pressure, and spinning dizziness (vertigo). This is common with fluid-related middle ear infections (otitis media with effusion) and Meniere's-like symptoms.

Treatment focus

Treatment aims to help drain the retained fluid and ease local swelling.

Pattern 03 Acute Inflammation / Flare-Up

When an acute inflammatory response from stress or infection concentrates in the ear, it can cause sudden pain, acute otitis media, or sudden hearing loss along with a high-pitched, metallic ringing.

Treatment focus

We focus on calming the acute inflammatory response.

Sudden hearing loss is a medical emergency. We strongly recommend seeing an ENT specialist right away — the early treatment window for sudden hearing loss is critical.

Nose

Nasal congestion, runny nose, sneezing, post-nasal drip, reduced sense of smell, nosebleeds. The nose is the first line of contact with the air we breathe, so it reacts fastest to temperature, humidity and allergens.

Pattern 01 Overactive Defense Response

When the membrane's immune response overreacts to cold air or allergens, it triggers immediate sneezing, clear runny nose and congestion. This pattern is common with allergic rhinitis.

Treatment focus

We aim to calm the membrane's overreaction and rebalance its protective function.

Pattern 02 Membrane Inflammation with Excess Discharge

When inflammation persists in the nasal membrane, it can lead to thick, yellow discharge, post-nasal drip, and sinus-type symptoms.

Treatment focus

Treatment focuses on settling the membrane inflammation and supporting drainage.

Pattern 03 Membrane Dryness

When the nasal membrane loses moisture, the inside of the nose can feel dry, crust easily, and bleed repeatedly. This relates to dry environments, aging, and chronic fatigue.

Treatment focus

We work to restore the membrane's ability to hold moisture.

Pattern 04 Reduced Local Circulation

When microcirculation beneath the nasal membrane slows, it can cause chronic congestion, thickened tissue changes, or a clear runny nose triggered by cold that won't let up.

Treatment focus

We aim to improve local circulation to help the membrane normalize.

Throat

Sore throat, globus sensation, dryness and itching, hoarseness, chronic pharyngitis, tonsillitis. The throat is a narrow shared passage for air and food, so it's constantly exposed to friction and irritation.

Pattern 01 Acute Inflammation

Irritating food, infection, or overwork can trigger a sudden inflammatory response with swelling, redness and sharp pain — this includes acute sore throat and tonsillitis.

Treatment focus

We focus on settling the acute inflammation quickly.

Pattern 02 Membrane Dryness & Depletion

When the vocal cords and throat membrane chronically lack moisture and nourishment, it can cause a dry cough without phlegm, a lingering sense of something stuck in the throat, and long-standing hoarseness. This relates to overuse of the voice, chronic fatigue, and aging.

Treatment focus

Treatment aims to restore the membrane's moisture and local nourishment.

Pattern 03 Functional Globus Sensation

Some people feel a persistent lump or blockage in the throat even though there's no actual mass or structural problem. We see this as nervous system tension from stress translating into local muscle tightness and heightened sensation.

Treatment focus

We work on easing the underlying nervous system tension. It's safest to confirm through a medical exam first that there's no structural cause before starting treatment.

Eye

Dry eye, eye strain, conjunctivitis, floaters, reduced vision, eyelid twitching, styes. The eyes are rich in fine blood vessels and made up of transparent tissue — the cornea, lens and vitreous — so they're highly sensitive to small shifts in circulation.

Pattern 01 Reduced Circulation Supply

When blood flow reaching the eyes isn't sufficient, it shows up as gradual eye fatigue, reduced vision, and blurriness that develops slowly over time.

Treatment focus

We aim to improve blood supply to the eyes.

Pattern 02 Acute Inflammation

A sudden inflammatory reaction in the conjunctiva or eyelid can cause redness, itching, discharge, and swelling — this includes conjunctivitis and styes.

Treatment focus

We focus on settling the acute inflammation.

For contagious viral conjunctivitis, we recommend combining treatment with careful hygiene and an ophthalmologist visit.

Pattern 03 Dryness & Depletion

Extended eye use or aging can reduce the membrane's ability to hold moisture, leading to dry eye, a gritty feeling, and a mix of relative redness and fatigue.

Treatment focus

Treatment aims to restore the membrane's moisture-holding capacity.

Pattern 04 Metabolic Buildup

When metabolic byproducts and micro-circulatory obstruction build up in the eye's clear internal tissue (vitreous, lens), it can lead to floaters or cataract-related changes.

Treatment focus

We work on improving local metabolism and circulation. Structural changes such as cataracts should be evaluated by an ophthalmologist first.

What Acupuncture & Herbal Treatment Actually Do

Across the ear, nose, throat and eyes, acupuncture and Traditional Eastern Medicine herbal treatment share the same underlying goals: improving local blood circulation, regulating the inflammatory response, restoring nervous system balance, and supporting the membrane's ability to hold moisture. In the acute phase, treatment focuses on settling inflammation; in the chronic phase, it shifts toward rebuilding circulation and membrane function. This is supportive care alongside — not a replacement for — medical treatment.

See a Specialist First

The following should always be evaluated by a physician before or alongside Traditional Eastern Medicine care:

  • Sudden hearing loss — seek emergency medical care immediately
  • Severe eye pain or sudden loss of vision — see an ophthalmologist immediately
  • Severe sore throat with high fever or difficulty breathing
  • Nosebleeds that keep recurring and won't stop
  • Chronic symptoms where an underlying condition (tumor, autoimmune disease, etc.) is suspected

Frequently Asked Questions

Can acupuncture completely get rid of tinnitus?
It depends on the cause and how long it's been present. Tinnitus from temporary circulation issues or stress tends to respond well, while tinnitus tied to long-standing nerve-related hearing loss more often becomes quieter and less bothersome rather than disappearing completely.
Can Traditional Eastern Medicine treat allergic rhinitis?
We work on regulating the allergic response itself and reducing the membrane's sensitivity. Rather than a cure, the goal is fewer and milder flare-ups, including around seasonal changes.
Why do tests come back normal for a lump-in-the-throat feeling?
Because it isn't a structural problem — it's a functional symptom (globus sensation) where nervous system tension affects local muscles and sensation. It's safest to start treatment only after testing confirms there's no structural cause.
Does acupuncture help with dry eye?
It can support tear production by improving circulation around the eyes and face and helping regulate the nervous system. We recommend combining it with basic care such as artificial tears.
Can floaters be treated?
Floaters come from changes inside the vitreous and are often difficult to eliminate completely. Improving whole-body circulation and metabolism can help slow their progression and ease eye fatigue. If floaters suddenly worsen or come with flashes of light, see an ophthalmologist right away.
How long does treatment usually take?
Acute symptoms (acute sore throat, acute conjunctivitis, etc.) are generally targeted for improvement within days to one or two weeks. Chronic symptoms (chronic rhinitis, dry eye, chronic tinnitus) usually need several weeks to a few months of steady treatment, since overall constitution and circulation need to improve together.
