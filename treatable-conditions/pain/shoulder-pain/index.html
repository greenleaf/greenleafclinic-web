Shoulder Pain: Treating the Tissue, Not Just the Diagnosis

"Frozen shoulder," "rotator cuff tear," "impingement syndrome" — these are just names. What actually determines your treatment is which tissue is involved, which movements are restricted, and whether the muscles that stabilize your shoulder have fallen out of balance.

The Same Diagnosis Doesn't Mean the Same Treatment

Two people can walk in with the same diagnosis and need completely different care. Shoulder pain isn't decided by a diagnostic label alone — "frozen shoulder," "rotator cuff tear," "impingement syndrome," "calcific tendinitis." What matters is which tissue is actually involved, which specific movements are restricted, whether the muscles that stabilize the shoulder joint have lost their balance, and whether the joint space and ligaments themselves are affected.

Because of this, every shoulder consultation starts the same way: identifying exactly which of these layers is driving your pain, then building the treatment plan — needle type, technique, and target — around that.

Matching the Technique to What's Happening in the Tissue

Shoulder pain generally falls into five recognizable tissue patterns, and each one calls for a different acupuncture approach.

01

Muscle knots and tender points

Acupuncture

The muscles around the shoulder — including the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis, deltoid, upper trapezius, levator scapulae, rhomboids, and pectoralis minor — become tight enough that pressing on them sends pain outward. Treatment places needles directly into the tight tissue to release the tension and restore blood flow to the area.

02

Deep or long-standing tension, with nerve involvement

Electroacupuncture

When tightness is too deep or too old to release with regular stimulation alone, or when it comes with tingling or radiating pain down the arm or hand, we add a mild electrical current to the needles. The rhythmic contraction and release reaches deeper muscle layers — like the infraspinatus and subscapularis — calms an irritated nerve response, and supports circulation.

03

Restricted movement, connective tissue that's bound down

Acupotomy

When lifting or rotating the shoulder is structurally limited — and fascia or connective tissue has essentially stuck together instead of gliding smoothly — regular acupuncture has little to work with. Acupotomy uses a needle with a microscopic blade at the tip to mechanically free the bound tissue and help restore range of motion. It's a form of acupuncture treatment, not surgery — no incision, no stitches, no anesthetic required. This is especially relevant for the joint-capsule adhesion behind frozen shoulder, adhesions within the rotator cuff fascia, and adhesions around the shoulder blade.

04

Poor circulation, a heavy or stagnant feeling

Wet cupping

When the shoulder feels generally heavy and a specific area seems to have poor local circulation, wet cupping can help. A small skin-level puncture is followed by cupping to draw out stagnant blood, improving local circulation and helping clear inflammatory byproducts. Some temporary bruise-like marking is normal afterward and usually fades on its own — particularly over the back of the shoulder and the inner border of the shoulder blade, where circulation tends to stagnate.

05

Joint or ligament-related pain

Acupotomy

Clicking or catching with movement, or a sharp stab of pain at a specific angle with tenderness where a ligament or tendon attaches to bone — as in impingement syndrome or calcific tendinitis — points to the joint itself rather than the surrounding muscle. Acupotomy is used again here, but with a different goal — instead of freeing bound fascia, the aim is decompressing the narrowed subacromial space and relieving built-up stress at the ligament or tendon attachment.

Why We Treat the Whole Rotator Cuff, Not Just the Sore Spot

The shoulder has the largest range of motion of any joint in the body — which is exactly why it's also one of the least stable. Four muscles work together to compensate for that instability: the rotator cuff.

Supraspinatus

Stabilizes the shoulder as you lift your arm

Infraspinatus

Rotates the shoulder outward

Teres Minor

Assists the infraspinatus with outward rotation

Subscapularis

Rotates the shoulder inward; stabilizes the front of the joint

Front tightens

Subscapularis and pectoralis minor shorten, pulling the shoulder joint upward into the acromion.

⇄

Back weakens

Infraspinatus and teres minor become overstretched, overtense, or lose strength.

In most shoulder pain, these two things happen together. If pain shows up when lifting the arm, it's often because a tight front pulls the joint upward into a collision with the acromion — and treating only the back muscles leaves that front tightness in place, so pain tends to return or never fully resolves. If pain shows up when rotating the arm inward, the tension and adhesion in the back muscles need attention too.

In practice, we check the tension and tenderness of both the subscapularis (front) and the infraspinatus and teres minor (back) at every shoulder visit. A short, tight front gets released; a weak back gets electroacupuncture to help restore strength; and acupotomy is used to release adhesion on both sides so the whole joint moves back into balance.

When Ligaments and the Subacromial Space Need Direct Attention

Some shoulder pain can't be explained by muscle alone. In these cases, the ligaments and the subacromial space — the space just beneath the tip of the shoulder blade — need to be treated directly:

Impingement Syndrome

Pain intensifies as you lift your arm, especially between about 60 and 120 degrees — a "painful arc." This happens because the subacromial space narrows and the supraspinatus tendon and its bursa get pinched against the acromion, causing inflammation. The key treatment is using acupotomy to decompress the subacromial space and clear fibrosis around the tendon attachment.

Frozen Shoulder (Adhesive Capsulitis)

The joint capsule itself thickens and adheres, so the arm won't lift or rotate properly. Because muscle release alone can't resolve a contracted joint capsule, acupotomy is used to release the adhesion between the capsule and the surrounding ligaments and restore range of motion.

AC Joint Sprain & Ligament Injury

Pain when bending the arm forward or pulling it across toward the opposite shoulder, with tenderness right at the tip of the acromion. This comes from tension or damage in the ligaments supporting the AC (acromioclavicular) joint, so acupotomy is used to decompress tension at the ligament attachment and support recovery.

Calcific Tendinitis

Calcium deposits form within the supraspinatus tendon, causing significant pain and restricted movement. Treatment focuses on improving blood flow to the deposit area, with acupotomy used to lower pressure around the tendon and support the body's own reabsorption of the deposit.

Biceps Tendinitis (Long Head)

Pain at the front of the shoulder that worsens when lifting the arm or carrying something. Treatment creates space in the bicipital groove — where the biceps tendon runs along the humerus — and uses acupotomy to decompress inflammation and adhesion at the tendon attachment.

In short: shoulder pain caused purely by muscle tightness usually responds well to acupuncture and electroacupuncture alone. But if you can't lift your arm, feel a sharp stab at a specific angle, hear or feel something catching when you move your shoulder, or your pain is worse at night, treatment targeting the ligaments and subacromial space with acupotomy should be seriously considered.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many acupuncture sessions does shoulder pain usually need?

Acute shoulder pain often improves within 1 to 3 sessions. Chronic issues like frozen shoulder, rotator cuff tears, or impingement syndrome typically need steady treatment, once or twice a week for 8 to 12 weeks or more. Restoring range of motion when there is significant joint capsule adhesion can take longer.

Can acupuncture help a torn rotator cuff?

It depends on the extent of the tear. For partial or small tears, acupuncture, electroacupuncture, and acupotomy can help rebalance the surrounding muscles and improve circulation, easing pain and supporting function. For large complete tears or significant strength loss, an orthopedic evaluation and possibly surgery may be necessary. We assess through palpation and a physical exam, and refer for imaging when appropriate.

Is acupotomy a form of surgery? Is it risky?

Acupotomy is not surgery, it is a category of acupuncture. The needle has a very fine blade at its tip that releases adhesions, but there is no skin incision, no sutures, and no local anesthesia involved. Mild soreness or bruising can occur afterward and usually resolves on its own. Because the shoulder joint has many nerves and blood vessels running through it, this treatment should only be performed by a clinician with thorough anatomical training.

How long does bruising from wet cupping last?

Bruising from wet cupping generally fades within 3 to 10 days, though this varies by person. More pronounced bruising in a particular spot can also reflect that local circulation wasn't moving well there. We recommend avoiding heavy alcohol or sauna use for about 24 hours after treatment.

Is it okay to exercise with shoulder pain?

During an acute flare, avoid strenuous exercise. Gentle stretching and pendulum exercises (Codman's exercises) are usually helpful. Once pain has settled, rotator cuff strengthening and shoulder blade stabilization exercises can be introduced gradually. Talk with your clinician about timing and technique. Frozen shoulder in particular often benefits from active range-of-motion work to help release the joint capsule.

My neck and shoulder both hurt, should they be treated together?

Often, yes. A large share of shoulder pain is connected to the neck. The trapezius, levator scapulae, and rhomboids all connect the neck and shoulder, so tension in the neck can worsen shoulder pain, and shoulder imbalance can, in turn, tighten the neck. Treating both together is usually important for preventing recurrence.

Can I get shoulder acupuncture while pregnant?

Acupuncture around the shoulder can generally be done safely during pregnancy. That said, the treatment area and intensity need to be adjusted based on your stage of pregnancy and overall condition, so please let us know if you're pregnant before treatment begins. Wet cupping and stronger acupotomy techniques are generally avoided during pregnancy.
