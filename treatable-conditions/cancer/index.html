Cancer & Chemotherapy

Support During Cancer Treatment

Cancer changes how your whole body regulates and repairs itself, and chemotherapy — a powerful, necessary treatment — adds its own toll on digestion, blood counts, and energy. Traditional Eastern Medicine works alongside your oncology team, focused on the specific symptoms you're dealing with right now.

Please Read First

Cancer needs to be diagnosed and treated by your oncology team — that isn't something Traditional Eastern Medicine replaces. Our role here is supportive: easing the physical toll of chemotherapy and helping your body hold onto its underlying capacity to recover. Please tell both your oncologist and our clinic about anything you're taking, and check timing with your medical team before starting treatment with us.

Two Different Kinds of Strain

We look at cancer and chemotherapy as two separate problems that call for different approaches. Cancer itself tends to build slowly — it reflects a gradual decline in the body's overall resilience, layered with more concrete issues like poor circulation, fluid buildup, and ongoing low-grade inflammation. Chemotherapy is different: it's a short, intense course of powerful medication that puts sudden strain on digestion, blood counts, and the nervous system. Our approach to each follows this same split — working with the underlying pattern behind the cancer on one hand, and helping your body recover from the acute impact of treatment on the other.

How We Look at Cancer

Two people with the same diagnosis can present very differently, depending on how far things have progressed and their overall constitution. The patterns below are the ones we see most often in clinic — in practice, they frequently overlap, and where you land depends on an individual assessment rather than the diagnosis alone.

Pattern 01 Circulation & Local Stagnation

Long-running stress or tension can leave blood flow congested around the affected tissue. This pattern shows up most often in earlier stages, or when pain is the dominant complaint.

A firm lump that stays fixed in one place
Sharp, stabbing pain that worsens at night
A duller, darker complexion
Rougher, drier skin
Treatment focus

Improving local circulation and easing tension in the tissue and its surroundings.

Pattern 02 Fluid & Lymphatic Congestion

When digestion has been running weak for a while, the body struggles to clear fluid and waste efficiently, and this tends to settle around lymph nodes or the thyroid.

A soft lump with an unclear edge
Little to no pain when pressed
A heavy, sluggish feeling in the body
Chest tightness; a swollen tongue with tooth marks along the edge
Treatment focus

Supporting fluid and waste clearance, and easing the congestion built up in the tissue.

Pattern 03 Acute Inflammation & Tissue Irritation

During periods when things are progressing quickly, tissue can become inflamed, ulcerated, or prone to bleeding. This is a stage where close, ongoing communication with your oncology team matters most.

Intense pain and heat at the affected site
Fever, and ulceration, discharge, or bleeding
A dry mouth with a bitter taste
Constipation and dark-coloured urine
Treatment focus

Calming the inflammation and supporting the tissue, in close coordination with your medical team throughout.

Pattern 04 Whole-Body Depletion

After a long illness, surgery, or a demanding course of chemotherapy, the body's overall capacity to recover can wear thin. This tends to show up in one of three overlapping ways:

  • Low energy and bloodA pale, dull complexion, exhaustion, shortness of breath, cold sweats, dizziness, and poor sleep.
  • Fluid depletion with low-grade heatThinness, warmth in the palms, soles, and chest, night sweats, a dry mouth and throat, and a dull, lingering ache at the affected site.
  • Slowed metabolism and circulationSwelling in the face and limbs, loose stools, a cold feeling in the abdomen, cold hands and feet, and weakness in the low back and knees.
Treatment focus

Rebuilding your baseline capacity to recover — the specific focus depends on which of the three patterns above is dominant for you.

How We Look at Chemotherapy Side Effects

Chemotherapy is a strong, fast-acting treatment, and the strain it puts on the body tends to move through in stages — first the digestive system, then blood and bone marrow, then overall metabolism, and finally liver and kidney function. We work with whichever stage you're in.

Pattern 01 Digestive Tract Irritation — Nausea & Vomiting

The most common early side effect, from chemotherapy drugs directly irritating the digestive tract.

Severe nausea and vomiting
Poor appetite
Bloating after eating
Frequent burping
Treatment focus

Calming the digestive tract's heightened sensitivity and easing the feeling of nausea.

Pattern 02 Digestive Decline & Fluid Retention — Diarrhea, Indigestion

As digestive function drops, the body has a harder time managing fluid, and this shows up as loose stools and swelling.

Watery or loose stools
Bloating, and nausea triggered by food smells
Swelling and heaviness in the arms and legs
A generally low, sluggish energy
Treatment focus

Restoring digestive absorption and helping the body normalize how it handles fluid.

Pattern 03 Bone Marrow Suppression — Anemia, Low White Cells & Platelets

Chemotherapy can affect the bone marrow's ability to produce blood cells. This stage depends heavily on your blood test results, so close communication with your medical team is essential throughout.

Severe fatigue and dizziness
A pale or sallow complexion
Heart palpitations
Easy bruising, bleeding gums, or tingling in the hands and feet
Treatment focus

Supporting the body's blood-forming capacity, guided closely by your bloodwork.

Pattern 04 Systemic Depletion — Fatigue, Low-Grade Fever, Dry Mouth

As chemotherapy continues, the body's fluids and reserves can wear down together — most noticeable during treatment or in the period right after it ends.

General weakness and low energy
Persistent dry mouth and throat, dry cough
Night sweats and low-grade fever
Mouth sores
Treatment focus

Rebuilding overall strength and fluid balance while helping manage the low-grade fever.

Pattern 05 Hair, Nerve & Reproductive Effects

Some chemotherapy drugs affect hair growth, peripheral nerves, and reproductive function — hair loss, neuropathy, and changes in fertility fall into this group.

Hair loss or slowed regrowth
Numbness, tingling, or altered sensation in the hands and feet
Weakness in the low back and knees
Irregular periods or reduced libido
Treatment focus

Supporting nerve recovery and tissue regeneration — how much and how quickly this responds varies from person to person.

Working With Your Oncology Team

Because chemotherapy schedules and blood counts change week to week, coordinating with your medical team is a constant part of how we work with cancer patients.

  • Traditional Eastern Medicine is a supportive approach, not a replacement for cancer treatment — whether and when to combine the two should always be discussed with your oncologist first.
  • Herb selection needs care around your chemotherapy schedule. Depending on timing — before an infusion, right after, or during a period of low blood counts — some herbs are adjusted or set aside because of possible interactions with your medication.
  • When white cell or platelet counts are low, we adjust acupuncture sites and technique accordingly.
  • Everyone responds differently. The patterns on this page are general references — your actual treatment plan is set through an individual consultation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Traditional Eastern Medicine treat cancer directly?

No. Traditional Eastern Medicine supports your body through the symptoms and side effects that come with cancer and its treatment. Diagnosing and treating the cancer itself needs to happen through your oncology team.

Can I receive treatment here while I'm going through chemotherapy?

Often, yes — but the right approach depends on your infusion schedule, your blood counts, and the specific drugs you're on. Please check with your oncologist before combining treatments, and let us know exactly what chemotherapy you're currently receiving.

Could herbs affect how well my chemotherapy works?

Some herbs can affect how the body processes medication, so we're careful about what we recommend during active treatment. Please tell us about everything you're taking — chemotherapy drugs, pain medication, and any supplements.

Does acupuncture help with chemotherapy side effects?

There's clinical experience suggesting it can help with nausea and vomiting, numbness or tingling from neuropathy, and fatigue. When platelet counts are low, we adjust the treatment sites and how firmly we work.

Can hair loss or neuropathy be addressed with Traditional Eastern Medicine?

Yes — we can work toward supporting tissue regeneration and nerve recovery. That said, how much and how quickly things improve varies a lot from person to person and depends on the specific chemotherapy drugs involved.

How long does treatment usually take?

It depends on where you are in your chemotherapy course — during infusions, right after finishing, or further along in recovery — and how significant your symptoms are. We reassess regularly and adjust the plan as you go.
