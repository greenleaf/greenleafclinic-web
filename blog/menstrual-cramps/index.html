Menstrual Cramps

Dr. Byoungjin Na, Dr.TCM — Director, Greenleaf Acupuncture & Herb Clinic

Mary had terrible menstrual cramps. Even with painkillers, the first and second days of her period always disrupted her regular daily activities, with dark, clot-heavy blood. Mary worked an office job and carried significant stress from work, struggling to find any sense of freedom there — likely tied to a perfectionist personality she readily acknowledged. Whenever frustrated or worried, she lost her appetite and had digestive trouble, and her eyes grew easily tired and dry, making afternoons especially hard to get through.

Three Questions for Stress Patients

When patients come in with stress-related issues, we always ask certain questions. First, about the chest — any tightness or tension, any deep-breathing problems or shortness of breath. Second, about dry mouth — distinct from simply drinking a lot of water, since many patients have a dry mouth even while drinking plenty, and we also check how moist the lips are (recommending lip balm for those with chronic dryness). Third, about dizziness — motion sickness on long car rides, discomfort in rear-facing SkyTrain seats, and tired or dry eyes.

Two or three of these three symptom clusters together point toward Radix Bupleuri — our most commonly used herb for stress, restoring blood circulation in the liver and facilitating gallbladder secretion.

Adding Uterine Circulation

In Mary's case, relieving stress alone wouldn't be enough to treat her menstrual cramps, since those cramps stem from poor uterine blood circulation — a separate issue requiring its own medicine. Many herbs promote uterine circulation, but we prefer Semen Persicae and Cortex Moutan, both easy to combine with other herbs and compatible with Radix Bupleuri.

Treatment

We gave Mary Radix Bupleuri for her stress, and Semen Persicae with Cortex Moutan for her uterine circulation. Relieving stress alone doesn't resolve menstrual cramps, and improving uterine circulation alone doesn't fully resolve them either — both aspects need mindful, combined treatment for the best outcome.
